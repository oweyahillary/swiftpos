# ⚡ ZapTill — Poster Design Rules & Principles

**The rules we follow when designing any ZapTill marketing poster.**

Drawn from established graphic‑design practice — hierarchy, typography, colour, accessibility, print —
and tuned for *our* posters: one product, Kenyan restaurant owners, shared mostly on phones.

## 🔒 Rules that never bend — brand guardrails

> - **Restaurant POS only** — market the tested restaurant POS; no other verticals or untested modules on public artwork.
> - **No pricing on the artwork** — pricing stays in the sales conversation.
> - **Only true, tested claims** — offline selling, M‑Pesa & split bills, tables/tabs, kitchen tickets, shift/day close, receipts, staff PINs.
> - **Artefacts stay local** — posters live in the repo; nothing published to any cloud host.

## 1 · One poster, one job

- **One message, one focal point, one CTA.** A poster that pushes several offers disorients the viewer. Pick a single
  value prop — *"M‑Pesa on every receipt"* **or** *"Keeps selling when the internet dies"*, never both.
- **Lead the eye with AIDA:** **A**ttention (focal point) → **I**nterest / **D**esire (the benefit) → **A**ction (the CTA).
- **The CTA is single, specific, verb‑led** — *"Book a demo →"*, not *"Learn more"* — in the accent colour, pill‑shaped,
  same on every poster.
- **Say *why*, not just *what*.** One line of meaning beats a feature name. Show the screen; don't describe it.

## 2 · Hierarchy — make the important thing obviously important

The classic shorthand **C‑R‑A‑P**, plus dominance:

| Principle | What it means on the poster |
|-----------|-----------------------------|
| **Contrast** | Make different things *clearly* different (size, weight, colour) — never slightly different. |
| **Repetition** | Reuse the same logo, colours and fonts across the set so posters read as one family. |
| **Alignment** | Every element shares an edge with something; nothing placed at random. |
| **Proximity** | Group related items; push unrelated ones apart with space. |
| **Dominance** | *If everything shouts, nothing is heard.* One headline wins; the rest supports it. |

## 3 · Typography — size for the thumbnail

- **Two typefaces max** — one display, one text. Ours: **Fraunces** (display) + **Figtree** (body); the emphasised word
  in *Fraunces italic* + accent colour is the signature.
- **Scale by distance** (≈ 1 inch of cap‑height per 10 ft):

  | Element | Role | Printed floor |
  |---------|------|:-------------:|
  | Title / hook | Biggest thing; legible at a glance | 50–72 pt+ |
  | Body / support line | Comfortably readable; never tiny | 24–32 pt |

- **The phone‑thumbnail test is our distance test.** If the hook dies when shrunk to a feed thumbnail or a WhatsApp
  status, it's too small or too busy.

## 4 · Colour — restrained, on‑brand, accessible

- **2–4 colours.** Lighten/darken one rather than adding a fourth that muddies the palette.
- **60‑30‑10** — ~60% dominant (often the background / negative space), 30% secondary, **10% accent → reserve it for the
  CTA.** Each poster may shout in its own bright palette, but keep this balance.
- 🔒 **Contrast** — body text **≥ 4.5:1** against its background; large text & key shapes **≥ 3:1**.
- **Never build hierarchy by greying text down** — it breaks legibility. Use size and weight instead.
- **Don't rely on colour alone** to carry meaning; keep strong figure/ground separation so each block reads as a shape.

## 5 · Layout, white space & grouping

- **White space does most of the work** — it's usually the bulk of your 60%. Crowding is the #1 thing that makes a
  poster lose meaning. Give the focal point room to breathe.
- **Aim for the feel:** generous space, strong visuals, lean text — not walls of copy.
- **Group with Gestalt:** proximity + similarity say *what belongs together*; size + weight + contrast say *what matters
  most*.
- **Use a grid and align to it** — don't centre everything by default; place the focal point deliberately.

## 6 · Imagery — show the real thing

- **Product‑forward** — show the real POS screen (tilted, glowing, with a status pill like *"Offline · 3 orders saved"*)
  and real Kenyan food. Don't describe what you can show. No stock clip‑art.
- 🔒 **High‑res: 300 DPI at final size** (150 DPI only for large format viewed from a distance). Never upscale.
- **Every image earns its place** — and you must have the rights to use it.

## 7 · Output & production

**Digital (default):**

| Artboard | Size | Use |
|----------|------|-----|
| **feed** | 1080 × 1080 | Instagram / Facebook feed |
| **story** | 1080 × 1920 | Stories / WhatsApp status |

Export RGB PNG + an HD variant.

**Print (🔒 hard specs, if a poster goes to a printer):**

- Build in **CMYK** (presses print CMYK; RGB shifts colour).
- **Bleed ≈ 3 mm / 0.125"** on any background that runs to the edge.
- **Safe margin ≈ 0.125–0.25"** — keep text and logo inside it.
- Export a press‑ready PDF, and **confirm bleed/margin against the printer's own spec sheet** — numbers vary by shop.

## ✅ Pre‑send checklist

Run this on every poster before it ships:

- [ ] A stranger gets the **one message** in ~3 seconds.
- [ ] **Exactly one** focal point and **one** CTA (accent colour, verb‑led).
- [ ] **Squint / blur test** — hierarchy still reads when you can't read the words.
- [ ] **≤ 2 fonts**, **≤ 4 colours**.
- [ ] Body text passes **≥ 4.5:1** contrast and isn't tiny.
- [ ] Shrunk to a **phone thumbnail** — the hook is still legible.
- [ ] Shows a **real** POS screen / real food at full resolution.
- [ ] **No pricing**, **restaurant‑only**, **only true claims**.
- [ ] If print: **CMYK, 300 DPI, bleed + safe margin** set.
