# ZapTill website: competitor analysis

Researched 21 October 2026 from public pages only. Nothing was signed up for, no forms were filled in, and no vendor was contacted.

**How to read this.** Section 1 is the five-minute version. Sections 6 and 7 are the parts to decide on. Wording from competitors is paraphrased. The few short quotes are attributed.

**Limits of the research.** Toast and Clover blocked automated reading (HTTP 403, or the page loaded only through JavaScript), so their notes rely on their own blog pages and on third-party reviews. The SimbaPOS, Square and Clover pages loaded as titles or menus only. Mobile layouts could not be seen in a text read, so mobile notes are limited to what the page structure shows. All prices are as published or reported on the date above; please re-check before quoting them to a client.

---

## 1. Summary: the 10 lessons that matter most

1. **Nobody in Kenya combines "works offline" with proof.** Uzapoint, Veira, Cute Profit and Pesapal never mention offline selling. Cute Profit even describes itself as pure cloud. NodePoint and Jampos say "offline", but neither shows a customer story. ZapTill's offline till plus branch server should be the opening promise, shown as a scene: *a power cut, a queue of sales saved, the till catching up.* NodePoint's outage picture is the best local example of this.
2. **Sell money and control, not features.** Every local site lists 15–25 features in grids. None tells an owner how much money they are losing today. ZapTill has the over/short-by-cashier report and money-leak analytics, so it can lead with *"Know where every shilling went."*
3. **Show the price.** About half of the Kenyan vendors hide their price (Uzapoint, Veira, iOSoft, Pesapal, SimbaPOS). The ones that publish (NodePoint, Cute Profit, Jampos, SokoniPOS) look more honest. Cute Profit publishes almost ZapTill's exact model (setup from KES 20,000 plus KES 2,500 a month), so publishing ours costs nothing.
4. **Make value for money concrete with a simple sum.** No Kenyan site offers a calculator or a "this pays for itself" sum. Yoco's fee slider and Toast's long list of case studies are the global benchmarks. A ZapTill "what is a short drawer costing you?" calculator would be unique locally.
5. **Real proof beats big claims.** Local sites are full of unproven absolutes such as "100% compliant", "zero leakage", "best in Kenya" and "4.9/5" ratings with no source. Uzapoint's impact counters even showed "0+" on the day we read it. One named Nairobi restaurant with one real number beats every superlative.
6. **WhatsApp is the expected front door.** Veira (a pre-filled WhatsApp message), Jampos ("book a demo on WhatsApp"), Cute Profit and NodePoint all use it. Make WhatsApp the main call to action, with "Book a visit/demo" second. A self-serve trial does not fit a product that needs setup.
7. **Pick a lane and say it.** The strongest sites name exactly who they are for: Toast says restaurants only, and TouchBistro even says who it is *not* for. Weak local sites try to be everything (iOSoft also sells SEO and web design; Uzapoint lists 100+ industries). Lead with hospitality: restaurants, cafés, bars and quick-service. Give retail and petrol their own pages.
8. **Show the real product.** The best pages show real screens: Cute Profit's stock deduction widget, Uzapoint's dashboard mock-ups and NodePoint's outage scene. Weak pages use emoji or stock photos. Use real ZapTill screenshots: the till, a kitchen ticket, the day close with over/short, the owner's phone summary and a "Do this next" card.
9. **Answer the fears in an FAQ.** The best FAQs (Lightspeed, TouchBistro, Loyverse, Cute Profit) handle the real objections: what happens offline, hardware cost, contracts, and who owns the data. TouchBistro is admirably candid about its offline limits. ZapTill should answer "What if the internet goes? Power goes? Can I leave? Who owns my data? Do you do eTIMS?" in plain words.
10. **eTIMS is the local hook everyone uses, and ZapTill should not pretend.** Veira, NodePoint, Cute Profit, Uzapoint and SokoniPOS all lead with KRA eTIMS. ZapTill has chosen to leave eTIMS to clients, so the site needs one honest FAQ line on it, and should compete on what others lack (offline, leak control, decisions) instead of matching them on eTIMS.

## 3. Per-vendor notes

### Kenyan and East African vendors

**Uzapoint** (uzapoint.com)
- **Who it speaks to:** African small businesses of every kind, plus resellers. There is a mission tone throughout ("built by Africans for Africans").
- **Structure:** hero, then product snapshot with HTML dashboard mock-ups, then proof (client names, partner logos for Safaricom, Visa and banks, awards with amounts), then packages, AI insights, a tax-compliance table, 100+ industries, an academy, and the footer. There is no FAQ.
- **Value:** AI claims such as "+18% revenue" and "2x SMS response", with no source.
- **Pricing:** hidden. The tiers have names but no amounts, and hardware says "Inquire".
- **Good:** realistic product mock-ups; awards that can be checked; M-Pesa everywhere, including M-Pesa's share of takings on the dashboard.
- **Bad:** the impact counters showed "0+" and "0%", which looks broken. Testimonials have no names. "AI" is hyped while the AI assistant is "coming soon". Offline is never mentioned.
- **Lesson:** a dashboard that looks like real data sells. Broken counters and AI hype cost trust.

**Jampos / QuickTill** (jampos.app). This is ZapTill's closest product rival: an offline desktop till that syncs.
- **Who it speaks to:** small retail and hospitality, with a Kenyan phone number and M-Pesa plus local banks (KCB, NCBA, Family).
- **Structure:** hero with a logo wall, then about 18 tool tiles, an AI "business score" lead magnet, M-Pesa, testimonials and an FAQ. The page is long and repetitive.
- **Tone:** hype and urgency ("N businesses signed up this week").
- **Pricing:** Rafiki / Jambo / Custom at $9.50 / $29.50 / $49.50 a month, with no unit or currency clarity. 14-day trial, no card needed.
- **Good:** separate sign-up paths for retail and hospitality; free tools (QR menu, barcodes) that bring in leads; "Book a free demo on WhatsApp".
- **Bad:** unproven "0% downtime" and "zero leakage" claims; emoji in place of screenshots; "24/7 support" contradicted by stated hours of Mon–Sat 8am–6pm.
- **Lesson:** ZapTill must out-prove Jampos on offline. Show the branch server keeping several tills in step, which Jampos does not claim.

**NodePoint** (nodepoint.africa). The best-built local site.
- **Who it speaks to:** shops, supermarkets, restaurants and hotels in East Africa.
- **Structure:** hero with a dashboard, then four modules **with prices on the first screen**, industries, an offline section with an outage picture, features, 3-step onboarding, pricing, the final call to action and the footer.
- **Pricing:** KSh 1,000 (Retail) / 1,500 (Restaurant) / 2,500 (Hotel) / 1,500 (Accounts) a month per licence, with unlimited devices. Annual billing saves about 17%. 7-day trial.
- **Trust:** a named legal entity; the promise that "your data stays yours" with CSV export even after you stop paying; security notes.
- **Bad:** no testimonials or logos at all; "100% features work offline" sits awkwardly beside eTIMS and STK push, which need a connection; "unlimited users" contradicts "5 staff accounts".
- **Lesson:** a clear, low price plus an offline scene is a strong combination. ZapTill's answer is depth (day close, over/short, branch server, analytics) and **real customers**, which NodePoint lacks.

**Cute Profit** (cuteprofit.com). The closest pricing model to ZapTill.
- **Who it speaks to:** restaurants, bars, cafés, bakeries and fast food in Kenya, including multi-branch groups.
- **Structure:** hero, then a "quick answer" block, kitchen tickets, M-Pesa, multi-branch, case studies, recipe stock, eTIMS, a comparison table, pricing, FAQ and the closing call to action. It runs logically from problem to proof to objections.
- **Value:** talks about stopping staff pocketing cash and kitchen theft, which is the same pain ZapTill solves, but with no numbers.
- **Pricing:** from **KES 2,500 a month**, cancel anytime, annual discount; **setup from KES 20,000**; extra branches billed; staff unlimited; hardware quoted separately; no transaction cut. No free trial (demo instead).
- **Trust:** "500+ branches" and "1,000+ businesses"; named case studies (TAMZ, Irama Cafe, Radiant Table); a Nairobi address; a Kenya Data Protection Act note.
- **Bad:** "pure cloud" with nothing on offline (its comparison table even knocks offline POS); a vague comparison table; an SEO note left by mistake in the FAQ.
- **Lesson:** this is the direct comparison a Kenyan restaurant owner will make. ZapTill is about KES 10,000 dearer on setup and KES 500 a month dearer, so the site must make the extra value obvious: offline, the branch server, over/short by cashier, and decision analytics.

**Veira** (veirahq.com)
- **Who it speaks to:** Kenyan small businesses; its customer logos are mostly retail, pharmacy and clinics.
- **Headline:** short and memorable (the guessing ends here), followed by a one-line list of jobs. A good pattern.
- **Calls to action:** "Get started" and **WhatsApp with a pre-filled message**. Very low friction.
- **Pricing:** hidden on the homepage. Third parties report about KES 2,000–5,000 per till. A handheld terminal is included on annual plans.
- **Bad:** a logo wall that mixes customers with Microsoft, KRA and Cloudflare (misleading); no offline mention; a weak page when JavaScript does not load. It also publishes many self-ranking "Top 5 POS in Nairobi" blog posts, which is good for search but thin on credibility.
- **Lesson:** copy the short headline and the pre-filled WhatsApp link. Do not copy the ambiguous logos.

**iOSoft Solutions / JiPOS** (iosoftsolutions.co.ke)
- **Who it speaks to:** hotels and restaurants, but the homepage sells "the best tech company in Kenya" plus SEO and web design.
- **Pricing and proof:** quote only, no trial, no demo button. "Guaranteed hotel growth" with no evidence. Duplicated paragraphs and typos.
- **Lesson:** a cautionary example. A product site must be about the owner's business, not about the vendor.

**Pesapal POS / Sabi** (pesapal.com)
- **Who it speaks to:** merchants who want M-Pesa plus card on one device. It also publishes guidance for petrol stations on eTIMS.
- **Trust:** strong. Central Bank of Kenya regulation, PCI badges and a physical address.
- **Weak:** no POS price, no offline mention, thin restaurant detail; it is really a payments company.
- **Lesson:** regulatory and security badges matter in Kenya. ZapTill can show "Safaricom Daraja integration" and its data-protection stance honestly.

**SimbaPOS** (simbapos.com). An established hospitality name, but the site loaded as a title only. Capterra lists a one-off US$249 and the press reports about KES 20,000 installation. The lesson is that an old local brand with a weak website leaves room for a challenger.

**Others noticed but not analysed in depth.** SokoniPOS (KES 1,500 a month, 14-day trial; its site sits on a temporary server domain, so it is not credible), RoyalPOS (an Indian product sold into Kenya), Paylink (payments, from KSh 1,000 a month; site down), and Olickhom (an airtime and M-Pesa service, not a real POS rival).

### African benchmarks

**Yoco (South Africa)**. The best African example.
- **Who it speaks to:** independent cafés, restaurants, retail and beauty businesses.
- **Headline:** runs your business, not just your payments.
- **Structure:** promo banner, hero, logo marquee, **three entry paths by goal** (get paid / run my business / build loyalty), products with prices, pricing, industries, testimonials.
- **Copy:** short lines that tie a feature to a real moment, such as nothing lost between the counter and the kitchen.
- **Pricing page:** the gold standard. R0 / R249 / R499 a month per location; a "Best value" middle tier; a 30-day trial on paid tiers; fee bands in a table; a **slider that shows your monthly cost from your sales**; struck-through bundle prices; an FAQ for objections.
- **Proof:** "250,000+ businesses"; named small-business testimonials (vague ones, repeated).
- **Weak:** too many calls to action; a banner discount that contradicts the product card.

**Orda (Nigeria, active in Kenya)**. The site was unreachable on the day. What we could see is proof through big named deals (EAT 'N GO chose Orda for 100+ Cold Stone and Pinkberry outlets in Nigeria and Kenya) and steady tech-press coverage (TechCabal). The lesson is that one well-known Kenyan client, announced properly, is worth more than a feature page.

### Global best-in-class

**Toast (USA)** (read through its blog and third-party reviews; the main pages blocked automated reading)
- **Who it speaks to:** restaurants only, in tight segments (quick service, full service, bars, cafés).
- **Proof:** a large customer-story library with numbers in the titles (for example "15% revenue boost"); industry reports used as lead magnets.
- **Pricing:** a "$0 starter kit" and a $69-a-month core plan are shown. But hardware (about US$2,600) and onboarding (about US$850–1,050) are extra, and reviews complain that a full set of add-ons runs to several hundred dollars a month.
- **Lesson:** stories with numbers sell. A "$0" headline with large hidden extras breeds distrust, which is ZapTill's opening to be the honest one.

**Square for Restaurants**
- **Pricing:** Free / Plus / Premium cards with a "Best value" tag, per-device add-ons (for example KDS at $20–30 a month per device), "Try free" on paid tiers, and custom pricing above US$250k a year.
- **Navigation:** split by restaurant type (coffee, quick service, bars, food trucks, bakeries).
- **Migration:** a "Switch to Square" path aimed at people leaving another system.
- **Lesson:** a migration promise ("we move your menu and stock for you") is a strong call to action.

**Lightspeed Restaurant**
- **Structure:** hero, then scale stats (200+ Michelin-starred restaurants, about 146K locations, 20 years), value pillars, a speed claim (40% faster, with a footnote), insights, modules, integrations, switching support, an FAQ and the footer.
- **Analytics:** sold as insight from every payment (menu optimisation, staff performance, benchmarks), but with no outcome numbers.
- **Pricing:** hidden ("request a quote").
- **Lesson:** analytics is now a headline theme for premium POS. ZapTill's ranked "Do this next" cards with KES estimates go further than Lightspeed's wording, so show one on the homepage.

**Loyverse**
- **Offer:** a free core POS; runs on your own phone or tablet; offline explained.
- **Proof:** stats (350,000+ merchants, 170+ countries) and one testimonial.
- **Calls to action:** "Start for free", no card.
- **Weak:** add-on prices hidden; the free-tier limits (for example 31 days of analytics history) appear only in the FAQ.
- **Kenyan relevance:** Loyverse is the "free" option Kenyan owners will compare against. ZapTill must explain why paid is worth it (M-Pesa, kitchen, day close, local support).

**TouchBistro**
- **Structure:** a sales phone number in the header; hero with "Get a quote" and "See pricing"; features; a "why us" section with three pillars; restaurant types; modules; integrations; stories; insights; FAQ.
- **Offline:** candid about the limits (orders and payments continue; reports pause).
- **Fit:** says plainly who it is not for (food trucks, retail).
- **Pricing:** "starts at" US$69 / US$99 a month, given in the FAQ.
- **Lesson:** honesty about limits builds trust. The site still has dead "#" links, which cost trust.

**Clover**. Only the page title loaded, so there is nothing usable.

## 4. Patterns that work

### Messaging
- **One outcome in the headline, the "what it is" in the sub-line.** Yoco, Veira and Loyverse do this best. A headline of six to nine words is the norm.
- **Name the moment, not the feature.** Yoco's lines tie features to moments on the shop floor (the counter-to-kitchen handoff, selling with no Wi-Fi). ZapTill equivalents:
  - "When KPLC goes, the till doesn't."
  - "Close the day in ten minutes, and know who was short."
- **Say who it is for.** Toast and TouchBistro are restaurant-first, and the clarity sells.

### Structure (the order that converts)
1. Hero: outcome headline, sub-line, WhatsApp plus a demo button, and a real product image.
2. Proof strip: named Kenyan businesses, branches and a number.
3. "Choose your path": Restaurant and café, Bar, Quick service, Retail, Petrol (as Yoco and Square do).
4. Three to five benefit blocks, each with a real screenshot.
5. Offline scene (as NodePoint does).
6. Owner-away block: the phone summary and the web dashboard.
7. Analytics teaser: one "Do this next" card.
8. Pricing summary with a link to the full pricing page.
9. Testimonials with numbers.
10. FAQ.
11. Final call to action (WhatsApp).

### Proof
- Customer stories with **a number in the title** (Toast).
- Named local case studies (Cute Profit) and a big named client (Orda).
- Regulatory and security badges (Pesapal).
- Scale stats (Lightspeed, Loyverse, Yoco). Use these only once they are real.
- **Avoid:** unproven absolutes, broken counters and ambiguous logo walls.

### Pricing presentation
- Public prices, in KES, with what is included in plain words (NodePoint, Cute Profit, Yoco).
- Say per what: per branch, per till or per licence. Confusion here is the most common fault (Jampos, Square).
- Show the annual saving (NodePoint about 17%).
- Label one option "Most chosen" or "Best value" (Yoco, Square).
- A calculator that turns the visitor's own numbers into money (Yoco).
- A pricing FAQ: contracts, hardware, cancelling, data export.

### Calls to action
- One primary call to action, repeated, and one secondary. Too many dilutes them (Yoco, Jampos, TouchBistro).
- In Kenya: **WhatsApp with a pre-filled message** (Veira) plus "Book a demo or a visit". A phone number in the header (TouchBistro) also suits the local habit.
- A lead magnet that is useful (Jampos's free tools, iOSoft's guide, Toast's reports). For ZapTill this could be a free "money-leak check" or a day-close template.

## 5. Gaps and opportunities in the Kenyan market

| Gap seen in local sites | Who shows the gap | ZapTill can own it by… |
|---|---|---|
| **Offline is missing or unproven** | Uzapoint, Veira, Cute Profit and Pesapal never say it; NodePoint and Jampos claim it without stories | Leading with it: a till that sells through internet and power cuts, a branch server that keeps every till in step, catch-up by itself. Show a scene and a customer quote. |
| **M-Pesa is a logo, not a workflow** | Most sites only say "accept M-Pesa" | Showing depth: Buy Goods and Paybill, split payments, M-Pesa matched to each sale, the number printed large on the receipt, and M-Pesa totals in the day close. |
| **No owner-away story** | Only Cute Profit hints at it | "Your daily summary on Telegram or email before you sleep"; every branch on one web dashboard; partner and accountant logins. |
| **Theft and leakage are mentioned, not measured** | Cute Profit and Jampos say "stop theft" with no mechanism | Showing the mechanism: drawer counts, **over/short by cashier**, wastage, credit ageing and manager confirmation for credit. Then put a number on it. |
| **No decision analytics** | Uzapoint has AI hype ("coming soon"); others have reports | Ranked "Do this next" cards with **KES estimates**, menu engineering and what sells when. Show a real card. |
| **Hidden or confusing pricing** | Uzapoint, Veira, iOSoft, Pesapal; Jampos's currency is unclear | Publishing KES prices and exactly what the setup fee covers. |
| **Shouting instead of proof** | "Best in Kenya", "100%", "zero", unsourced ratings | Calm, specific claims and named clients. |
| **Contact is form-first** | iOSoft, Pesapal, Lightspeed-style "request a quote" | WhatsApp first, a real person, a Nairobi phone number, a promise to answer the same day. |
| **Generic or imported tone** | Hype (Jampos), buzzwords (Uzapoint), SEO filler (iOSoft) | Warm, plain Kenyan English. Talk about the drawer, the kitchen, the night shift, shillings and KPLC. |
| **No hospitality-specific depth** | Veira and Pesapal have little restaurant detail | Showing a kitchen ticket, dispatch ticket and kitchen display, plus combos and variants on the receipt. |
| **Branding per branch is not offered** | None seen | "Your name and logo on every till and receipt, per branch." |
| **Petrol stations are ignored** | Only Pesapal writes for them | A dedicated petrol page (once the feature set supports it fully). |
| **Support and updates are unclear** | Vague "24/7" claims (Jampos contradicts itself) | Saying what really happens: automatic updates, and support can pull logs remotely, so most issues are fixed without a visit. |

**Where ZapTill is weaker, and how to handle it honestly**
- **eTIMS.** Competitors lead with it. Give one plain FAQ line on how ZapTill clients handle eTIMS today, and do not overclaim.
- **Windows only.** Loyverse, Veira and NodePoint run on phones and tablets. Frame Windows as "a proper counter till that does not depend on a phone signal", and mention Android as coming only when it is real.
- **No self-serve trial.** Replace it with "We'll set you up and train your staff" (a done-for-you setup), which justifies the setup fee.

## 6. Recommended positioning for ZapTill (3 options)

All three speak from the owner's side (money, control, peace of mind) and keep the technology in the supporting lines.

### Option A: Never stop selling
> **Headline:** Your shop keeps selling, even when the internet doesn't.
>
> **Sub-headline:** ZapTill runs your tills, kitchen and M-Pesa through outages and power cuts, then catches up on its own. Every shilling is counted, every branch is in view, and you can see it all from your phone.

*Best if:* offline reliability is the main reason clients pick ZapTill. It is the clearest differentiator against Cute Profit, Veira and Uzapoint.

### Option B: Know where every shilling went
> **Headline:** Know where every shilling went, every single day.
>
> **Sub-headline:** M-Pesa matched to each sale, drawer counts by cashier, and stock that adds up. ZapTill closes your day in minutes and tells you who was short, before you go to sleep.

*Best if:* the target is owners who suspect leakage (bars, busy restaurants, multi-branch groups). It sets up the Analytics upsell naturally.

### Option C: Run it from anywhere, and grow
> **Headline:** Run your business properly, even when you're not there.
>
> **Sub-headline:** ZapTill keeps the till, the kitchen and the stock in order while you're away, sends you the day's numbers each night, and tells you what to change next to make more money.

*Best if:* the buyer is an owner of two or more branches, or an investor-owner with a manager running the place. It carries the "Do this next" analytics story best.

**Suggestion:** use **B as the homepage headline** (money and control is the strongest emotional hook, and no competitor owns it), **A as the first benefit block** (the proof that ZapTill is different), and **C for the multi-branch and Analytics pages**.

## 7. Pricing-page recommendations

**Current price:** KES 30,000 setup, then about KES 3,000 a month (KES 36,000 a year). Analytics is planned at about KES 10,000 a month.

**Where this sits in the market**

| Vendor | Setup | Monthly | Year one |
|---|---|---|---|
| NodePoint (restaurant) | none stated | KSh 1,500 | about KSh 18,000 |
| Cute Profit | from KES 20,000 | from KES 2,500 | about KES 50,000 |
| **ZapTill** | **KES 30,000** | **about KES 3,000** | **about KES 66,000** |
| Toast (USA, for scale) | about US$850+ onboarding plus hardware | US$69 and up | far higher |

ZapTill is at the upper middle of the Kenyan market. That is fine for a premium product, but the page must justify the gap.

### Recommendations
1. **Publish the prices in KES** on a dedicated pricing page and as a one-line summary on the homepage.
2. **Explain what the KES 30,000 setup buys**, item by item. For example:
   - an on-site install;
   - your menu and stock loaded for you;
   - printers and kitchen screens set up;
   - staff roles and PINs created;
   - training for cashiers and managers;
   - branding on the till and the receipt;
   - the first day-close done together.

   This turns a fee into a service. Cute Profit says nothing about what its KES 20,000 setup covers, which gives ZapTill an edge.
3. **Say exactly what the monthly fee is per**: per branch, per till or per business. Recommended: **per branch**, with tills included up to a stated number, because owners hate per-terminal maths. State what is included: updates, support, the web dashboard, daily summaries and remote fixes.
4. **Show the annual option as the default** ("KES 36,000 a year, or KES 3,000 a month"). Consider a small reward for paying yearly, such as one month free or a reduced setup fee, as NodePoint and Cute Profit do.
5. **Two or three clear packages:**
   - **ZapTill** (core): till, M-Pesa, kitchen, day close, stock, staff, dashboard, daily summaries.
   - **ZapTill Online** (the premium licence that already exists): images, variants, offers and stock on desktop. Name it so owners understand what they get.
   - **ZapTill Analytics** (about KES 10,000 a month): menu engineering, what sells when, money leaks by cashier, credit ageing, and "Do this next".

   Tag the middle package "Most chosen".
6. **Frame the monthly cost as a daily cost:** KES 3,000 a month is **about KES 100 a day**. Put it next to the cost of the problem: "if one cashier is short KES 200 a day, that is KES 6,000 a month."
7. **Add a simple "what is it costing you?" calculator.** The visitor enters daily sales, the number of cashiers and a guessed shortage. The page shows a monthly leak in KES against ZapTill's monthly price. It is unique in Kenya and inspired by Yoco's slider. Keep it honest: label it an estimate and use the visitor's own inputs.
8. **Sell Analytics with its own payback logic.** Show one real "Do this next" card with a KES estimate. Offer a **first-month money-leak report** (one-off or free with an annual plan) so the owner sees a number before committing to KES 10,000 a month.
9. **Be the honest one.** Add a short "No surprises" block:
   - no percentage of your sales;
   - M-Pesa charges are Safaricom's, not ours;
   - hardware is quoted separately, or bring your own;
   - cancel with notice;
   - your data is yours to export.

   This directly counters the reputation of "$0" offers with large hidden extras.
10. **Pricing FAQ:**
    - Do I need internet?
    - What hardware do I need, and can I use my own?
    - What happens if I stop paying?
    - What about eTIMS?
    - How long does setup take?
    - Can I add a branch later?
    - Is there a contract?
11. **Calls to action on the pricing page:** "Chat on WhatsApp" (primary) and "Book a visit" (secondary). No self-serve trial is needed; instead offer **"See it running at a shop near you"** or a demo.

## 8. Proof ZapTill should start collecting now

Nothing on the site will matter as much as three named Kenyan businesses with a number each. Start this month.

**Numbers to capture from existing clients** (with permission, and compared with the time before ZapTill):
- **Over/short:** the total drawer variance per month before and after, or "shortages caught in the first month: KES X".
- **Offline:** "Hours sold during outages this year" and "Sales saved while offline". The till data can count these automatically. Consider a small internal report.
- **Speed:** minutes to close the day, before and after.
- **Stock:** wastage as a share of sales, before and after; stock-take time.
- **M-Pesa:** the time spent matching M-Pesa to sales each day (often "an hour gone to zero").
- **Analytics:** one "Do this next" recommendation acted on, and the result in KES.
- **Uptime and support:** updates shipped automatically; median time to fix through a remote log pull.

**Testimonial formats**
- **A short WhatsApp voice note or 30-second phone video** from the owner, in their own words (Kenyan English or Sheng is fine). Transcribe it for the site.
- **A quote plus a number plus a name, business and town.** For example: *"[Name], owner, [Café], Westlands: our shortages dropped from about KES X to Y a month."*
- **Photos of a real counter** with the ZapTill till and the kitchen printer, with the owner's permission.

**Case studies to write first (one page each):**
1. **A multi-branch restaurant or quick-service group:** branch server, the web dashboard and per-branch branding.
2. **A busy bar:** over/short by cashier, credit accounts with manager confirmation, the night close.
3. **A café or quick-service shop with frequent power cuts:** the offline story.
4. (Later) **a retail or petrol client**, when ready.

Use this structure for each: the problem, then what changed, then **one headline number**, then a quote.

**Other trust assets**
- A logo strip of **real** clients only (ask permission), never vendor or partner logos mixed in.
- "Built in Nairobi" with a real address, phone number and company registration.
- A data stance: where the data lives, who can see it, export on request, and the Kenya Data Protection Act.
- A "We've been running since…" fact and a count of tills and branches live, once it is impressive.
- Google Business reviews. Ask every happy client for one.
